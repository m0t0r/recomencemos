# Operability advisory — 0002 profile-to-contact-exchange

The draft carries 25 NFRs, and **24 of them are build-time assertions**. NFR7 (oldest undelivered Offer ≤ 24 h) and NFR25 (magic-link acceptance) are the only two that describe a running system, and neither names who learns when it breaks. So the honest summary is: this spec is exceptionally well specified and almost entirely unobservable. Every number can be proven true in CI on the day it merges and no number can be shown still true a week later.

Three facts compound: logs are undrained, deploys are continuous on one machine, and nobody is on call. Together they mean **the forensic record of an incident is destroyed by the next deploy, which happens several times a day, and nobody is looking anyway.**

## Recommend

- **An availability SLI and target for the two public reads, because none exists.** `/` and `/perfiles`, measured by an external prober, not by the machine itself. Propose **99.5% monthly** (≈ 3.6 h). This is not arbitrary: a single Fly machine cannot roll — it stops and starts — so at ~30 s of boot-and-healthcheck per deploy and 10 deploys/day, deploys alone spend **~0.35%, an implicit 99.65% ceiling** the spec never states. Either state the ceiling or remove it (see the next item).
- **`deploy.strategy = "bluegreen"` in `fly.toml`, gated on an HTTP health check.** This is the highest-leverage single decision in the effort. It removes the per-deploy outage, and — because a failing health check aborts the deploy and leaves the old machine serving — it is the **only rollback that works when nobody is watching**. NFR24's "undoable in ≤ 5 minutes by a documented command" assumes a human who noticed. Automatic gating assumes nothing. (Verify the strategy name and single-machine behaviour against current Fly docs; I could not.)
- **A `GET /api/health` route, absent from the API contract.** Without one, Fly's check degrades to TCP-accept, which succeeds while every route returns 500 — so a broken deploy passes its health check and replaces a working machine. It must do a trivial DB round-trip (else a bad `DATABASE_URL` deploys clean), respond in **≤ 50 ms**, carry `noindex`, and return no data. It is also the uptime monitor's target.
- **Turn on the Sentry uptime monitor — the free tier includes exactly 1** (runbook §3, pinned 2026-08-23). Point it at `/api/health` at a 1-minute interval, alerting after 2 consecutive failures. Detection in **~2 minutes** versus "when a Worker mentions it to someone" is the entire difference this product can afford. It is free and requires no code.
- **The drain is not deferrable.** The intent puts "wiring the log drain" out of scope as a deploy-time act. Under continuous deployment the current log retention is **not 30 days, it is until the next deploy** — minutes. NFR17's "from the moment a drain exists" is doing a lot of work. Cheapest route needs no new vendor: `enableLogs: true` + `Sentry.pinoIntegration()` (runbook §9b), **5 GB/month free ≈ 370k lines/day** at ~450 B per JSON line, which this product will not approach. `error.levels` defaults to `[]`, so it does not break report-once. Recommend this lands in the same PR as the first deploy.
- **`NEXT_PUBLIC_RELEASE` set from the commit SHA in CI (runbook §2), stated in NFR24.** With ~10 deploys/day, the first question about any incident is "did the last deploy do this?" Unset, every log line reads `release: "unknown"` and that question is unanswerable — while Sentry events still carry a plugin-injected release, so the two halves disagree and it looks like it works.
- **Replace NFR25's SLI. It is the wrong indicator and will go green while nobody can sign in.** Provider-accepted and hard-bounce rates say nothing about a brand-new sending domain landing in Colombian Gmail/Hotmail spam — accepted, not bounced, never read. Since magic link is the *only* key to an account, that failure is total and silent. The indicator close to the user is **conversion**: share of `requestMagicLink` calls followed by a completed sign-in within 30 minutes. Propose **≥ 70%, rolling 7 days**, and treat a **drop of >20 points against the trailing 30-day value** as the actionable signal rather than the absolute level. It costs two `info` lines (link requested, link consumed), both id-only, so NFR18 is untouched. Keep the bounce rate as a secondary.
- **A saturation signal on the human queue, which NFR7 cannot provide.** Age-of-oldest is a *latency* SLI and it moves slowly. An adversary sending 400 Offers in an hour leaves age-of-oldest under 24 h for most of a day while the queue becomes unholdable by one person. Add **depth > 50 unreviewed items, or > 20 Offers arriving in a rolling 1 h window**. This is also the only detector this design has for a coordinated abuse campaign.
- **Name the saturating resource. I count three, and the machine-level one has a bug in it today.** (a) **Machine memory** — `uploadPhoto` buffers multipart in-process; propose a **1 GB** machine floor and a **2 MB** photo cap with browser-side downscale to ~1600 px before upload (which also serves NFR3's mobile-data premise). (b) **The Postgres connection pool** from one machine to PlanetScale — cap it explicitly and state the number; every cold start reopens it. (c) **The Admin's daily attention**, which NFR7 and the depth band above measure.
- **Promote story 22 out of `Could`.** The age-of-oldest display on every Admin screen is currently the *only* mechanism by which NFR7's band is detectable, and it sits in the lowest tier. A `Could` story is the detector for a `Must` NFR.
- **An operational leg on the announcement gate.** The gate is currently "every `Must` ticket closed" — purely functional. The announcement *is* a deliberate traffic spike, aimed at one machine in `iad`, with a cache that empties on every deploy, and the attention window is the intent's scarce resource. Add: drain collecting, uptime monitor firing, rollback rehearsed once against production, and a load test showing `/` and `/perfiles` hold NFR2 at **50 req/s sustained and 500 concurrent**. Announcing onto an unmeasured machine is how this product spends its one window on a 502 page.

## Risks

- **Nothing in this design measures the user's experience of latency, anywhere, ever.** NFR2 is server-side on the `iad` machine — that is measuring your comfort, not hers; it excludes the ~90–110 ms Bogotá↔`iad` RTT plus TLS. NFR3's LCP p75 is user-side but is a *lab* number under synthetic throttling, and analytics/RUM are explicitly out of scope. So a regression that doubles real Worker-side page load is invisible to every instrument this spec names. If the region is still movable, **`mia` cuts roughly 40–60 ms of RTT off every Worker request** versus `iad` while keeping acceptable numbers for globally-distributed Hirers — the Worker side is the one on constrained connections.
- **`uploadPhoto` as specified fails for essentially every real photo, and I verified this rather than recalled it.** Next 16.3.2's bundled docs (`apps/web/node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/05-config/01-next-config-js/serverActions.md:29`) state the Server Action body limit defaults to **1 MB**, and `apps/web/next.config.ts` does not set `experimental.serverActions.bodySizeLimit`. A phone photo is 2–5 MB. The operability consequence is worse than the bug: this fails at the framework boundary, so it will surface as an opaque failure with no domain log line, on the one flow (NFR4) that is already the fragile part of publishing.
- **NFR21's attention-spread bound is enforced on the wrong surface and nothing checks the realized outcome.** The ordering lives on `/perfiles`; the Wall (`/`) is newest-first, and the Wall is the indexable, most-linked, most-shared surface — so the majority of Hirer traffic will bypass the spread mechanism entirely. Testing Decisions tests the *ordering function* against a generated population, which can pass while the production distribution violates ⌈3 × M/N⌉ badly. The SLI has to be the realized distribution: max delivered-Offers share to one profile over a rolling 7 days, computed weekly and shown in the Admin queue. Left as-is, the fairness property this product is partly built on is asserted and never observed.
- **`fly secrets set` restarts machines, so this effort has no "seconds" rollback class at all.** The runbook's table lists config changes as seconds; on Fly with one machine a secret change is a restart — the same class and blast radius as a redeploy. And no feature-flag mechanism exists here, so "behind a flag" is unavailable as a mitigation. Every mitigation this design has is redeploy-class or slower, which makes the bluegreen health gate above the only fast one.
- **NFR24's ≤ 5-minute rollback stops being true the moment a migration ships in the same deploy as the code that needs it,** and this effort introduces the repo's first database. `migration-policy` is `UNSET` in `docs/policy/data.md`. Rolling back the code leaves a schema the old build does not understand. Also unstated: *where* migrations run. In a Fly `release_command` a failed migration aborts the deploy with the old machine still serving; at server boot it takes the site down. That is the difference between a non-event and an outage nobody is watching.
- **The genuinely one-way action in this design is a send, and no forward fix is written down.** A bug that delivers an Offer, or an `ExchangedContact`, to the wrong recipient cannot be rolled back — the email is in an inbox and the phone number is on a stranger's screen. NFR11 correctly states the *deletion* asymmetry; nobody has stated the *mis-send* one. The forward fix needs to exist before it is needed: a kill switch on `@repo/notifications` that stops all sends, a log line per send carrying `exchange_id` and recipient *id* (never address) so the affected set is enumerable afterward, and a written notification procedure. Without the per-send line the blast radius is not even knowable.
- **The spec says what must never be logged (NFR18) and never says what is logged.** Every safety-relevant transition in this system — Offer delivered / rejected by Admin, photo approved / rejected, Report created and the synchronous freeze (NFR15), Block created, sessions revoked, Account deleted, ContactExchange created — has no record outside mutable database state. When an Admin has to reconstruct an abuse incident, or answer a Ley 1581 *reclamo* about what happened and when, there is nothing to read. Recommend an explicit `info`-level event list with a stable `event` field and ids only, which satisfies NFR18 by construction.
- **Rate-limit refusals and any throwing 404 will burn the Sentry error quota, and a spent quota means the second incident of the month is invisible.** 5,000 errors/month, dropped not billed (runbook §3–4). `requestMagicLink` is rate-limited on a public unauthenticated endpoint; if a refusal throws rather than returning, a crawler or a script spends the month's allowance in a day. The API contract's `{ ok: true }`-always shape is right for enumeration, and the same discipline needs stating for the refusal path.
- **The free tier includes 1 cron monitor and this effort introduces at least three scheduled jobs** — the seven-day check-ins, the NFR17 retention purges, and NFR21's rotation. A purge that silently stops running is a growing Ley 1581 exposure with no symptom. Simplest resolution: one heartbeat job that runs all three and reports once, so one monitor covers all three.

## Concerns

- [ ] **C-OP1** — No availability SLI or target for the public read paths. Propose **99.5% monthly** on `/` and `/perfiles`, measured by the external uptime monitor, not server-side.
      **Risk if wrong:** the site is down and the first person to learn is a Worker who assumes she did something wrong; with nobody on call, detection latency is the entire mitigation.
      **Owner:** On-call lead. **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `default-availability`
- [ ] **C-OP2** — Where a breach lands. Propose two destinations, because the two failure classes differ: a **daily digest email at 08:00 America/Bogotá to the Admin via the existing Resend seam** for the human-queue bands (NFR7, queue depth), and a **`needs-triage` GitHub issue via the CI workflow** for machine bands (uptime, error spike), per ADR-0001 and runbook §6. Neither needs new infrastructure.
      **Risk if wrong:** every band in this spec stays two-thirds of a band — metric and range with no "who learns" — which intent Q3 explicitly said this effort should not ship.
      **Owner:** On-call lead. **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `alert-destination`
- [ ] **C-OP3** — The consequence of a spent budget. With one unpaid person the only real lever is deploy cadence. Propose: **when >50% of the 30-day availability budget is spent, deploys pause except fixes and cadence drops to once daily until it recovers.**
      **Risk if wrong:** continuous deployment with no on-call and no brake is a system that degrades monotonically with nobody empowered to stop it.
      **Owner:** On-call lead. **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `error-budget-policy`
- [ ] **C-OP4** — The inherited latency default. Propose **p95 ≤ 400 ms server-side and ≤ 1200 ms user-measured** for public reads, matching NFR2 and making explicit the network leg NFR2 currently excludes.
      **Risk if wrong:** the next spec re-derives a number, and NFR2 continues to read as a user-experience commitment while measuring only the machine.
      **Owner:** On-call lead. **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `default-latency`
- [ ] **C-OP5** — NFR24 settles `rollback-mechanism` as "≤ 5 minutes by a documented command," but does not name the classes. Propose recording **code-only = bluegreen redeploy of the previous image, ~3 min; additive migration = same, the column stays; a completed send = one-way, forward fix only**; and forbidding a contracting migration in the same deploy as the code change (expand/contract).
      **Risk if wrong:** the stated 5 minutes is true for the easy case and false for the two that matter, discovered mid-incident.
      **Owner:** On-call lead (with Data lead). **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `rollback-mechanism`
- [ ] **C-OP6** — NFR17 sets logs to 30 days "from the moment a drain exists," and no drain exists. Propose **the drain lands in the same PR as the first deploy**, making 30 days true from day one rather than a future tense.
      **Risk if wrong:** under continuous deployment, real retention is until the next deploy — minutes — so no incident is diagnosable after the fact, and the effort's own logging NFRs are unverifiable.
      **Owner:** On-call lead (with Data lead). **Unblocks by setting:** `docs/policy/operability.md` → `log-retention` (mirror `retention-logs` in `data.md`)
- [ ] **C-OP7** — Deploy strategy and the migration execution point on a single machine. Propose **bluegreen, health-gated, migrations in a `release_command`**.
      **Risk if wrong:** every deploy is a small outage and a bad deploy replaces a working machine, several times a day, with nobody watching either event.
      **Owner:** On-call lead (with Repo owner, who owns CI).
- [ ] **C-OP8** — Region. Propose **`mia` over `iad`** for ~40–60 ms of RTT on every Worker request, or record `iad` as accepted with the cost named.
      **Risk if wrong:** the users on the most constrained connections pay a latency tax no instrument in this spec would ever report.
      **Owner:** On-call lead.

## Handoffs

- **data-design** — I need the migration reversibility answer (`migration-policy`) to close C-OP5. Where data-design marks anything one-way, the forward fix is mine to name; my candidate one-way list is the mis-send (an email cannot be recalled) and the photo object-storage delete, not the schema. I also need the Postgres connection cap to make "saturation" a number rather than a word, and confirmation that the NFR17 purge jobs are enumerable so one cron heartbeat can cover them.
- **ux-design** — NFR2 and NFR3 are latency SLOs on user-visible paths, so they depend on the loading and error states committed to. Specifically: what the Wall shows during the 60-second cold window after each deploy (NFR2's 800 ms allowance is only defensible if the state is a skeleton rather than a blank), and what a Worker sees when `uploadPhoto` fails at the framework body limit — today that is an unhandled boundary failure with no copy and no log line.
- **security-design** — the photo size cap is a saturation control and an abuse control at once; I propose 2 MB from the memory side and expect a converging answer from the abuse side.

## Not applicable

- **Multi-region, replication, and failover.** `hosting-target` fixes one machine; there is no second thing to fail over to, so RPO/RTO is a data-design and hosting question rather than a band.
- **Queue, worker, or job-runner instrumentation.** Every consequential transition in this design is synchronous and in-transaction (NFR15 deliberately so). The only asynchrony is scheduled sends, covered by the cron-monitor note.
- **Feature-flag rollback.** No flag mechanism exists in this repo, so the class is unavailable rather than unchosen — which is why the bluegreen health gate carries the weight.
- **Payment, settlement, and reconciliation monitoring.** ADR-0007; the platform holds no money, so there is no financial invariant to band.
- **Client-side error volume.** Session replay and user feedback are not installed (runbook §3), so they consume no quota and contribute no signal. `logging.browserToTerminal` is a dev-loop convenience and is not observability.

## What I could not check

Said explicitly, because a silent gap reads as a clean bill of health.

- **Fly.io behaviour** — that `bluegreen` is available on the target plan and behaves as described with one machine, that `release_command` runs before the new machine takes traffic, and that `fly secrets set` restarts machines. All three are from recall, and all three are load-bearing in the recommendations above.
- **PlanetScale for Postgres connection limits** on whichever plan is chosen, so my "cap the pool" recommendation carries no number.
- **Resend's free-tier daily/monthly send caps and its webhook event vocabulary** — NFR25 depends on both, and a send cap hit during the announcement spike would break sign-in exactly when it matters.
- **Actual `iad`↔Pereira RTT.** The 90–110 ms and the `mia` saving are estimates, not measurements. They are worth one `ping` before the region is fixed.
- **Whether Sentry's free tier still includes 1 uptime and 1 cron monitor.** The runbook pins those figures at **2026-08-23**, two days before this reading; its own instruction is to re-check §3 before relying on it, and two of my recommendations rely on it.
- **The other advisories.** I am isolated by design and have not tried to guess what security, data, or simplicity will say. Where simplicity argues against the health endpoint or the second Fly machine, that is a real trade and the Tech lead arbitrates it — my position is that the health check is the cheapest item on this list and the one that makes every other mitigation work.
